Interval cheat-sheet

1 semitoneminor 2nd — the smallest step on the piano, always between a white key and the black key beside it
2major 2nd — like C to D, two keys apart with one in between
3minor 3rd — the sound of a sad chord
4major 3rd — the sound of a happy chord
5perfect 4th — the first two notes of "Here Comes the Bride"
7perfect 5th — the most stable interval; root + 5th alone is a power chord
10minor 7th — what turns a plain chord into a bluesy, dominant one
11major 7th — dreamy, jazzy, one semitone below the octave
12octave — the same note, higher

Three rules that build almost every chord

Stack in 3rdsCount up the scale in thirds (C E G B D F A). Root + 3rd + 5th is a triad; add the 7th, then the 9th, 11th and 13th for richer chords.
The 3rd decides moodMajor 3rd (4 semitones) = bright. Minor 3rd (3 semitones) = dark. Every chord symbol either says maj, says m, or says nothing — nothing means major.
Extensions stack upward7 → 9 → 11 → 13. A 9th chord already contains the 7th; a 13th chord contains the 7th, 9th and 11th. Symbols only name what you need to notice.
